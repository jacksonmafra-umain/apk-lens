# Plain-language summary

Use last. For a reader who will never open a report file.

---

Rewrite the analysis for someone who is not technical and is deciding whether
to keep this app installed.

## Constraints

- No jargon. No API names. No package paths. If a term is unavoidable, define
  it in the same sentence.
- Under 400 words.
- Lead with the answer, not the method.
- Be specific about uncertainty in ordinary language: "the app is built to do
  X" is honest; "the app does X" is not, when nobody watched it run.

## Structure

1. **The short answer** — two or three sentences.
2. **What it can see about you** — a list a person would recognise: where you
   are, who your contacts are, which apps you have, what you look at.
3. **Who else is involved** — how many other companies' code is inside, and
   what that kind of company does.
4. **What it does not do** — the reassuring negatives, plainly. This matters:
   without it the summary reads as an accusation.
5. **What nobody can tell you from this** — that no one watched the app run, so
   this describes what it is *built* to do.
6. **What you can actually do** — the two or three platform controls that
   change something (deny a permission, reset the advertising ID, restrict
   background activity).

## What to avoid

- Do not use the word spyware, or any word implying intent. This method cannot
  establish intent, and the summary that reaches a non-technical reader is
  exactly where an overstatement does the most damage.
- Do not imply the app is unusual if the findings are typical of its category.
  If they are typical, say that — it is the most useful sentence in the summary.
- Do not end on fear. End on the specific things the reader controls.
